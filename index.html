PROBLEM STATEMENT CS-02

Autonomous Phishing Email Triage (VCET Hacksprint 24H)

"Employees report hundreds of suspicious emails a day, far more than a security team can manually triage, and treating every borderline case with a coin-flip decision either misses real threats or buries inboxes..."

Why PhishGuard wins: Most competing teams build a naive binary classifier that forces a guess. PhishGuard's answer addresses what the system does when it is unsure. Clear cases resolve instantly; borderline cases undergo automated Tier 2 investigation, grouping duplicates into single campaign incidents with full auditability.

Autonomous Triage Architecture

PhishGuard replicates the decision triage workflow of an emergency room: fast sorting for clear cases, specialist diagnostic tests for borderline cases.

Tier 0 · Fast Rules & Auth

Runs instantaneously on 100% of reported emails. Evaluates email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), internal corporate allow-lists, and high-confidence IOC threat feeds.

Tier 1 · Calibrated ML Model

Trained probability classifier powered by scikit-learn & LightGBM. Calibrated so a 90% score truly means 90% accuracy, allowing policy thresholds to be fully trusted.

Tier 2 · Specialist Investigators

Deploys only when Tier 1 is borderline. Dispatches specialist agents: Header/Sender Agent, Look-Alike Domain Scanner, and Message Intent Parser to construct deep evidence.

Arbiter & Policy Engine

Synthesizes multiple evidence signals into a transparent 3-state verdict: Phishing (Quarantine), Benign (Release), or Needs Review (Finished Case File).

Campaign Grouping & Compression

Collapses 30+ identical phishing reports into a single actionable SOC incident using lexical vector similarity and shared root domains. One analyst decision resolves the entire wave.

Safety Guardrails & 1-Click Undo

Autonomous actions only quarantine—never delete—with instant 1-click reversal. High-impact operations on VIP/Executive mailboxes require human authorization first.

1. The Project in One Minute

MAILSENTRY is an autonomous phishing email triage system for security operations teams. Security teams receive hundreds of employee-reported suspicious emails every day, far too many to review one by one.

Today's tools force a yes-or-no verdict on every email, including the ones they are unsure about. A wrong "safe" lets a real attack through. A wrong "phishing" buries someone's inbox.

Core principles
  • Cheap checks first. Every reported email goes through fast rules first. Clear-cut emails are resolved automatically.
  • Unclear emails are not guessed. The system runs deeper Tier 2 investigation and hands the analyst a finished case file: verdict, calibrated confidence, evidence and reasons.
  • Campaign grouping. Duplicate reports of the same attack are grouped into one incident, so it is handled once.

Why this approach: most solutions are a classifier that says phishing or not. Our focus is what the system does when it is unsure, which is the failure named in the problem statement.

2. The Problem Statement (CS-02)

The challenge is titled "Autonomous Phishing Email Triage". The statement visible to us reads:

"Employees report hundreds of suspicious emails a day, far more than a security team can manually triage, and treating every borderline case with a coin-flip decision either misses real threats or buries inboxes i…"

The source text is cut off. Everything below is built from the visible part.

Signal in the statement What it means for MAILSENTRY
"hundreds of suspicious emails a day" The system must handle volume automatically through fast-path tiers, not classify one email at a time.
"far more than a security team can manually triage" The target user is the security team. Success is measured by how much manual work we remove.
"coin-flip decision" on borderline cases The centre of the problem. Borderline emails need an evidence-based path, not a forced guess.
"misses real threats or buries inboxes" Both errors matter: false negatives (missed attacks) and false positives (legitimate mail blocked).
"Autonomous" (in the title) The system acts on its own where it is safe, with human control where risk is high.

4. Our Solution: "Investigate, Don't Guess"

Think of a hospital emergency room. A nurse sorts patients quickly: some need immediate treatment, some can go home, and for the unclear ones tests are ordered before a decision is made. MAILSENTRY works the same way, with three tiers of increasing effort.

Tier What it does Speed Runs on
Tier 0 · Fast rules Checks known-bad senders and links, the company allow-list, and email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC). Instant Every reported email
Tier 1 · Calibrated ML A LightGBM / scikit-learn model outputs the probability that the email is phishing. "Calibrated" means a 90% score is right about 90% of the time, so thresholds can be trusted. Very fast Emails Tier 0 could not settle
Tier 2 · Investigators Specialist checks gather evidence: sender/header check, link and look-alike domain check, and message-intent check. Slower, deeper Only borderline emails

Worked examples

  • Example 1. "Verify your payroll now" from paypa1-secure.com, failed authentication, known-bad link. Tier 0 and Tier 1 agree it is malicious. Result: auto-quarantine, block the link, notify the reporter.
  • Example 2. Monthly newsletter from a trusted vendor with valid authentication. The Tier 0 allow-list matches and Tier 1 returns a very low score. Result: auto-release as safe, notify the reporter.
  • Example 3. "Urgent: update your bank details" from a new sender with clean grammar and a normal-looking link. Tier 1 returns about 55%, a coin-flip. Tier 2 checks sender history, header mismatch, link destination and intent. Result: evidence decides, either phishing with reasons or Needs Review with a full case file.

5. Decision Policy & Safety Guardrails

Decision thresholds

Situation Outcome
Calibrated phishing probability ≥ 0.90 and at least two independent signals agree Auto-quarantine. Block the link or sender, notify the reporter.
Probability ≤ 0.10 and the sender is authenticated Auto-release as benign, notify the reporter.
Anything in between, or only one signal fired Tier 2 investigation, then re-score.
Still unresolved after Tier 2 Needs Review. Goes to the analyst queue with the full case file.

These numbers are starting values. They are tuned on the validation set, and the final values are the ones we report.

Safety guardrails

  • Reversible actions only. Quarantine, never delete, with a one-click undo.
  • Human approval for high-impact mailboxes. Executive and VIP mailboxes need analyst sign-off.
  • Rate limit and kill switch. Auto-actions can be paused instantly from the dashboard.
  • Audit log. Every decision stores its inputs, evidence, verdict and model version so it can be replayed and explained.

Browser extension Adds a "Report phishing" button on webmail and sends sender, subject, body text and links to the server. Also scans links on any page.

8. Data Strategy & Validation Sets

Data work is led by Praveen Kumar M.G. Every dataset is checked for licence and availability before use. Candidates are public phishing email corpora (for example Nazario), the SpamAssassin corpus, and Enron for legitimate mail, plus public phishing URL feeds.

Test set Purpose
Standard train / validation / test Baseline accuracy. Near-duplicates are removed first to avoid leakage.
Hard set (about 150 to 300 emails) Borderline cases that break simple classifiers: urgent but legitimate HR/IT mail, real password resets, aggressive marketing, and well-written phishing.
Adversarial set Modern AI-written phishing, look-alike domains, QR-code links, and benign look-alikes.

What we measure

  • Precision, recall, F1, and false-positive rate at fixed recall.
  • Calibration: reliability curve and expected calibration error (ECE), which show whether thresholds are meaningful.
  • Tier funnel: percentage resolved at Tier 0, at Tier 1, at Tier 2, and sent to a human.
  • With vs without Tier 2 on the hard set, the chart that tests our core idea.
  • Campaign compression: reports divided by incidents. Analyst time saved uses an assumed manual minutes-per-email, stated openly as an assumption.
Reporting rule: No number appears in the deck, website or pitch unless it comes from our own evaluation. Targets are not results.

14. Limitations & Honest Scope

MAILSENTRY is a 24-hour hackathon prototype for first-level triage. It supports analysts and does not replace them.

Area Current state Not included (roadmap)
Results Every performance figure comes from our own held-out evaluation. Decision thresholds are starting values tuned on a validation set. Production-scale or long-term measurements.
Data Public corpora (Nazario, SpamAssassin, Enron) and public URL feeds. They may not match any one organisation's mail. Training on an organisation's own mail.
Mailbox integration Demo uses a mock inbox, the extension, a paste page and .eml replay. Live Microsoft 365 or Gmail integration.
Investigation Tier 2 covers header and URL checks, with message-intent analysis as a further check. Sandbox link detonation and a live red-team generator.
Learning Models are trained offline before the demo. Analyst feedback retraining and a client-PC agent.
Autonomy Auto-actions are reversible. VIP and high-impact mailboxes need human approval. A kill switch pauses all auto-actions. Fully unattended operation.
Reused work Extension and dashboard approach come from our DeepShield project. None. The triage pipeline, policy engine and campaign grouping are new.

15. Glossary & Definition of Done

Glossary

  • Triage: sorting incoming reports by risk and deciding what to do with each.
  • Borderline / coin-flip case: an email the system cannot confidently call safe or malicious.
  • Calibration: making a model's confidence honest, so 0.9 is right about 90% of the time.
  • SPF / DKIM / DMARC: email authentication checks that show whether a sender is who they claim to be.
  • Campaign: many reports that are really the same attack sent to many people.
  • Quarantine: moving an email out of the inbox into a safe holding area. It is reversible.
  • False positive / false negative: safe mail wrongly blocked / a real attack wrongly allowed.
